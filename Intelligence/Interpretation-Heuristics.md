## 0. First principles — the extraction curve

Everything reduces to **how much soluble material** you pull, and **how evenly**.

- **Extraction yield (EY):** % of the ground coffee dissolved into the cup.
  - **Under ~18%** → sour, salty, **hollow**, thin — the sweetness hasn't developed yet.
  - **~18–22%** → the classic sweet/balanced window.
  - **Above ~22%** → bitter, drying, **astringent** — you've gone past sweetness into harsh compounds.
  - *Light, dense, evenly-brewed coffees can push the upper bound higher; dark roasts turn bitter earlier.*
- **Order of extraction:** acids & fruit dissolve **first**, then sugars/sweetness, then bitter/drying compounds **last**. Sweetness lives in the *middle* — you must extract far enough to reach it, without tipping into the bitter tail.
- **Evenness beats aggression:** an uneven bed extracts some grounds too much and others too little at the same time — the worst of both (astringent *and* sour). Even extraction is usually worth more than any single parameter.

**The five levers** (how you move along the curve): **grind, temperature, time/contact, agitation, ratio.** Water chemistry sets the ceiling. Strategy = choosing which levers to pull, and which to hold back.

---

## 1. The levers — direction of effect

| Lever | More extraction / effect | Risk if pushed too far | Best used to… |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Grind finer** | ↑ surface area → ↑ EY, ↑ body | clogging, channelling, astringency, fines | add energy *without* heat — the precise lever |
| **Temp higher** | ↑ EY, ↑ sweetness/body | blows past delicate aromatics; bitterness on dark roasts | extract dense/light roasts; the blunt lever |
| **Time / contact ↑** | ↑ EY | over-extraction, flat/bitter tail | reach sweetness on hard-to-extract coffees |
| **Agitation ↑** | ↑ EY, ↑ evenness *and* ↑ fines migration | astringency, muddiness, silt | boost extraction when a coffee is *forgiving* |
| **Ratio (more water)** | ↑ EY potential, ↓ strength (TDS) | thin/watery if overdone | raise extraction while lightening body |
| **Bypass (water added post-brew)** | dilutes strength (TDS) without changing extraction yield | cup becomes thin/watery if overdone | fine-tune strength after extraction is dialled in; every 10g bypass reduces TDS by ~0.2% |
| **Water GH ↑ (Mg/Ca)** | ↑ extraction ceiling | over-extraction, harshness | give flat water more pulling power |
| **Water KH/buffer ↑** | mutes/rounds acidity | dull, lifeless cup | tame aggressive acidity (rarely wanted for clarity) |

Rule of thumb the mentor uses: **grind is the scalpel, temperature is the hammer.** Prefer the scalpel when protecting aromatics.

## 2. Coffee observations → interpretation

### Altitude / density
| Observation | Mechanism | Extraction implication |
|---|---|---|
| High grown (≥1,700 masl) / SHB | slower cherry maturation → **denser, harder cell structure** | lower solubility → **needs more energy** (finer / hotter / more contact); more forgiving of a wide window |
| Low grown (<1,100 masl) | softer, more porous beans | extracts easily → back off energy; over-extraction risk |

### Roast level
| Observation | Mechanism | Extraction implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Light** | dense, less-developed, less soluble; acids intact | **hardest to extract** → finer grind, hotter water (93–96°C), even bed; under-extraction (sour/hollow) is the main risk; sweetness sits deep in the curve |
| **Medium** | balanced development & solubility | widest usable window; most forgiving |
| **Dark** | porous, brittle, highly soluble; sugars caramelised/degraded | **over-extracts fast** → coarser, cooler (88–92°C), gentle; bitterness/ash is the risk; body high, acidity low |

### Processing method
| Observation | Mechanism | Extraction implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Washed** | mucilage removed pre-dry → clean, transparent | acid-forward, **lighter body**, high clarity ceiling → clarity/floral plays; punishes over-agitation with astringency |
| **Natural** | dried in cherry → fruit/ferment sugars in bean | **more body & sweetness**, lower clarity; muddiness/ferment-funk risk → manage evenness, often slightly gentler/cooler |
| **Honey / pulped natural** | partial mucilage | between washed and natural; balanced body & sweetness |
| **Anaerobic / carbonic** | fermented under controlled/low-O₂ | intense, volatile aromatics; **fragile** → cooler water, gentle handling to keep it clean, not boozy |

### Combinatorial hierarchy (Process × Roast)
Roast defines the extraction ceiling (density); Process defines the extraction speed (solubility). When they conflict, Process takes priority for grind size and temperature; Roast takes priority for contact time and agitation.

- Light + Washed → hardest to extract. Grind: finest of all. Temp: 94–96°C. Contact: standard (2:30). Agitation: moderate (needs energy, but protect clarity).
- Light + Natural → porous but dense. Grind: medium-fine (not as fine as washed). Temp: 92–94°C (cooler to avoid over-extracting fruit sugars). Contact: standard. Agitation: gentle (muddiness risk).
- Medium + Natural → very forgiving. Grind: medium. Temp: 90–93°C. Contact: standard. Agitation: moderate.
- Dark + Washed → soluble but clean. Grind: medium-coarse. Temp: 88–90°C. Contact: shorter (2:00–2:15). Agitation: minimal (astringency risk).
- Dark + Natural → fastest extracting. Grind: coarse. Temp: 88–89°C. Contact: short (under 2:00). Agitation: very minimal (muddy/bitter risk).

### Roast age (degassing)
| Observation | Mechanism | Extraction implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Very fresh (<7 days)** | heavy CO₂ off-gassing | CO₂ repels water → uneven extraction, gushing blooms → **longer/multiple bloom**, expect to grind finer as it settles |
| **Peak (~7–21 days filter)** | degassing stabilised | most predictable; brew to plan |
| **Aging (>4–6 weeks)** | aromatics oxidising, staling | flat, papery → slightly hotter/finer to compensate, but manage expectations |

### Variety & producer (context, lower weight)
| Observation | Mechanism | Extraction implication |
|---|---|---|
| Geisha / delicate florals | prized volatile aromatics | protect with cooler water & gentleness; clarity-first |
| Bourbon / Caturra / typica | classic sweetness/body | reward mid-curve extraction for sweetness |
| Named quality producer / competition lot | likely well-sorted, even roast | supports precision; smaller adjustments matter |

### Stated tasting notes
Treat as the **target the roaster believes is achievable** — a hypothesis to extract *toward*, and a check against the user's result. Floral/tea-like notes ⇒ protect aromatics (gentle, cooler). Syrupy/chocolate/caramel ⇒ chase sweetness (reach mid-curve). Bright citric/berry ⇒ acidity structure present; don't bury it with buffer.

## 3. Setup observations → interpretation

| Observation | Mechanism | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Flat-burr grinder** (EK43, Ode) | narrow particle distribution | even extraction; can go slightly finer without astringency |
| **Conical hand grinder** (Comandante, 1Zpresso) | some fines, portable | solid clarity; watch fines-driven astringency |
| **Entry grinder** (Encore, blade) | wide distribution, more fines | expect unevenness → don't over-extract; agitation caution |
| **Flat-bottom brewer** (Kalita, Ode-style) | even, forgiving bed | consistent; good for sweetness/body |
| **Plastic brewer (V60 plastic)** | low thermal mass, retains heat well | slurry stays hot; use standard target temp |
| **Ceramic / Metal / Glass brewer** | high thermal mass, steals heat from slurry | slurry drops 2–4°C during brew → compensate by starting +2°C hotter than the standard recommendation for the same coffee |
| **Conical brewer** (V60) | fast, pour-sensitive, high ceiling | clarity/aromatics; rewards even pouring, punishes sloppiness |
| **Immersion** (Hario Switch, Clever) | full contact, high body | body/sweetness; clarity limited by fines |
| **Thicker paper filter** | slows flow, scrubs fines | more clarity, less body |
| **Water GH low / RO / distilled** | little to extract with | flat, weak → needs remineralisation; interpret flatness as *water*, not technique |
| **Water high alkalinity (KH)** | buffers acids | dull, muted acidity → interpret lost brightness as *water* |
| **Batch size larger** | deeper bed, longer drawdown | tends to raise extraction/body → adjust grind coarser as size grows |

## 4. Radar priorities → strategy levers

### Baseline-to-gap discipline

Radar values are desired outcomes, not parameter commands. First estimate what the context-built baseline will produce, then optimise only the material gap. Direct credible cup feedback overrides model assumptions. Defects take precedence: correct sour/hollow/uneven cups before pursuing clarity, and correct bitter/dry/muddy cups before increasing extraction. Prefer one calibrated grind step for a moderate, credible gap; use two only for a high gap with supporting evidence. Add one supporting lever only where the primary move cannot safely serve the objective.

### Progressive radar interpretation

- Preserve raw 0–100 priorities. Interpretation zones are 0–20 strongly deprioritised, 21–40 mildly deprioritised, 41–59 neutral, 60–79 moderately prioritised, and 80–100 strongly prioritised.
- Use a smooth influence curve across zone boundaries; a one-point move cannot cause a large recipe jump. Relative weights are not normalised to an arbitrary sum.
- Resolve combinations before levers: clarity+floral through controlled flow; clarity+body through concentration plus fines control; sweetness+acidity through even extraction and suitable water; body+juiciness through lively texture; floral+body through ratio/filter trade-offs.
- Conflict notes require both priorities to be extreme, an equipment constraint, and a materially weak compromise. Attempt an equipment-specific compromise first.
- Clarity does not imply coarser grind; body does not imply cooler water; sweetness is not a fixed extraction stage; floral does not require a fixed bloom or lower temperature; acidity is distinct from sourness; juiciness has no universal ratio.

The six radar axes are the user's **constraints**. Each maps to how strategy should lean (Stage 3), not to fixed numbers.

| Priority ↑ | Sits where on the curve | Lever lean | Deliberate trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sweetness** | mid curve (reach it, don't pass it) | slightly finer / a touch more contact; even bed | needs precision — easy to overshoot into bitter |
| **Clarity** | clean, controlled extraction | even pours, moderate agitation, thicker filter, sometimes coarser | often costs body |
| **Body** | fuller extraction / less filtration | finer or immersion, more contact, metal/looser filter | can cost clarity; muddiness risk |
| **Acidity** | earlier curve, preserve acids | cooler water, brighter/faster, low-buffer water | can read sour if under-developed sweetness |
| **Floral** | protect volatile aromatics | cooler water, gentle handling, fresh grind | costs some extraction depth/body |
| **Juiciness** | sweetness + acidity + texture together | higher dose, even bed, mid temp | the hardest to maximise — a balancing act |

**Conflicts are the interesting part.** Clarity + Body pull opposite ways; Floral + high Sweetness create the classic "protect aromatics *and* reach the sugars" tension. Naming the conflict *is* the strategy (Stage 3), and the judgement (§4 of the master) is where the AI takes a side.

**Resolving a conflict with method, not compromise** *(principle PRN-0001, active)*: when the user wants **Clarity *and* Sweetness** together — normally a trade-off — reach for a **valve-controlled two-stage brew** (percolation, then immersion) rather than splitting the difference. Percolate first with the valve open to extract acids and aromatics with fresh moving water (clarity, brightness); then close the valve and immerse, letting **contact time** reach the mid-curve sugars (sweetness, body) without added agitation. The two goals develop on separate clocks instead of competing in one continuous pour. *Requires a switch/valve brewer; pairs with a coarser grind (PRN-0003, candidate) so time — not fineness — does the extracting.* Sources: SRC-0001 (Nas Jaafar, 2026 WBrC) + SRC-0002 (Lume, "Juicy Pour Immersion") — two independent switch-staged brews (confidence 0.68).

## 5. Risk patterns — what's likely to go wrong

| Coffee signature | Dominant risk | Pre-empt with |
|---|---|---|
| Light + high altitude + washed | under-extraction (sour, hollow) | more energy via grind, even bed, adequate temp |
| Delicate washed / floral | astringency from agitation | gentle pours, fewer/softer agitations |
| Natural / anaerobic | muddiness, ferment funk | evenness, slightly cooler, clean pours |
| Dark roast | over-extraction (bitter, ashy) | coarser, cooler, gentle, shorter |
| Very fresh | uneven bloom, gushers | extended/multiple bloom |
| Entry grinder / wide distribution | simultaneous over+under (astringent+sour) | target evenness first; avoid extremes |

## 6. Diagnosis map — tasted defect → cause → correction

Powers Stage 6. Read the *cup*, name the *mechanism*, prescribe the **smallest** effective move.

| Tasted | Most likely cause | First correction (smallest lever) |
|---|---|---|
| **Sour, sharp, lacking sweetness** | under-extraction | grind finer 1–2 steps; then ↑ temp 1–2°C |
| **Hollow / empty middle** | under-extraction (sweetness not reached) | grind finer; slightly longer contact |
| **Bitter, harsh** | over-extraction | grind coarser; ↓ temp; ↓ agitation |
| **Astringent / drying** | over-agitation or too fine → fines | gentler pours; slightly coarser; fewer agitations |
| **Stalled drawdown (brew stops draining) + muddy bed + mixed sour & bitter taste** | fines migration + clogged filter (over-agitation or grind too fine) | Grind coarser (not finer) AND reduce agitation (fewer pours, lower flow rate, no swirl) — this fixes the physical stall without over-extracting the fines |
| **Muddy / unclear** | fines, uneven bed, over-agitation | coarser or better pour discipline; thicker filter |
| **Thin / watery** | under-extraction or over-dilution | finer; or lower ratio (less water) |
| **Flat / dull / lifeless** | water buffer too high, or stale coffee | lower-alkalinity water; check roast age |
| **Weak but not sour** | low strength, extraction ok | lower ratio (more coffee / less water) |
| **Boozy / fermenty (naturals)** | too hot / uneven on a volatile lot | ↓ temp; gentler, even pours |

**One change at a time.** Two simultaneous adjustments make the next brew uninterpretable — the mentor moves the single highest-leverage lever and re-reads.

## 7. How these heuristics are used (and not)

- They are **reasoning inputs**, surfaced only as plain-language teaching — the user sees "finer grind, because a dense light roast needs more energy," never a lookup table.
- They are **directional**, always overridden by the **user model** (this grinder runs coarse; this user likes it brighter) from `Extraction-Intelligence-Layer.md` §7.
- They compose: real coffees trigger several rows at once, and the strategy resolves the tensions between them.
- They are **living** — the internal repository and brewing history refine these defaults over time. When a lot behaves against type, the coffee model wins and the AI says why.
