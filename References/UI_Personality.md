# Brew Helper — UI Personality

**Type:** The dashboard's character — its voice, temperament, and manners
**Reads with:** `Visual-Principles.md` (Design System)

> Visual Principles governs how it *looks*. This governs how it *behaves and speaks* — the felt personality behind the pixels. When a copy line, empty state, or interaction feels off, this is the file it failed.

---

## 1. Who Brew Helper Is

**A World Brewers Cup coach standing beside you at the counter.**

Experienced, calm, and generous with knowledge — the kind of person who has pulled ten thousand shots and can tell what your cup needs from a sentence of description. They don't lecture. They notice, they guide, they explain *why*. They believe you can get better, and they make the next step obvious.

Not a barista showing off. Not an app cheerfully gamifying your morning. A quiet expert who is genuinely on your side.

---

## 2. Personality in Five Traits

**1 · Calm.** Never rushed, never loud. Unhurried confidence. The interface breathes; so does the voice.

**2 · Confident.** States things plainly. "Grind finer next time" — not "You might perhaps consider a slightly finer grind." A coach commits.

**3 · Warm.** On your side. Encouraging without flattery. Celebrates progress quietly, never with confetti.

**4 · Precise.** Specialty-coffee literacy. Uses the real vocabulary (extraction, agitation, clarity) but never to gatekeep — always to teach.

**5 · Curious.** Treats every brew as an experiment worth learning from. Framing is "let's find out," not "you got it wrong."

---

## 3. Voice & Tone

**Voice (constant):** knowledgeable, warm, economical. A trusted expert who respects your time.

**Tone (shifts by moment):**

| Moment | Tone | Example line |
|---|---|---|
| Welcome / Hero | Inviting, open | "What are we brewing today?" |
| Bean analysis | Observant, teaching | "This is a delicate washed coffee — it rewards clarity over body." |
| Setting the goal | Collaborative | "What do you want from this cup?" |
| Recommendation | Confident, clear | "Start here. This recipe leans into the sweetness you're after." |
| After a rough brew | Encouraging, matter-of-fact | "A little hollow in the middle — that's under-extraction. Let's fix it." |
| Diagnosis | Explanatory | "Grind finer to slow the flow and pull out more sweetness." |
| Community | Companionable | "Others brewing this bean landed here too." |

---

## 4. How It Speaks — Rules

- **Short over long.** One confident sentence beats a paragraph. Cut hedges ("maybe," "you could try," "it seems").
- **Second person, present tense.** "You'll taste more sweetness." Direct, immediate.
- **Explain the why, briefly.** Every recommendation earns a one-line reason. That's the coaching, and it's optional-to-expand, never forced.
- **Sensory, not clinical.** "Hollow," "juicy," "muddy," "bright" — the language of tasting, not spreadsheets.
- **Never blame.** Brews aren't failures; they're data. "That's under-extraction" not "you under-extracted."
- **No exclamation-point cheer.** Warmth comes from being genuinely helpful, not from punctuation.

---

## 5. Emotional Design — What It Should Feel Like

- **Entering:** like walking into a calm, well-lit café where someone competent has time for you.
- **Setting up a brew:** like a conversation, not a form. Each question earns the next.
- **Getting a recommendation:** relief and trust — "okay, I know what to do."
- **After brewing:** understood, not graded. Curiosity about the next attempt, not anxiety about this one.
- **Over time:** quiet mastery. The product should make the user feel like they're *becoming a better brewer* — the compounding satisfaction of a craft.

---

## 6. Personality Anti-Patterns

- ❌ **Cheerleader app** — "🎉 Great job! You brewed a coffee!" No. Respectful, not childish.
- ❌ **Know-it-all** — condescension, jargon-as-gatekeeping. The coach teaches, never talks down.
- ❌ **Nervous assistant** — over-hedging, over-apologising, burying advice in qualifiers.
- ❌ **Corporate neutral** — bland, voiceless SaaS copy. Brew Helper has a point of view.
- ❌ **Data robot** — reporting numbers without meaning. Always translate data into taste and next action.

---

## 7. One-Sentence Test

Before shipping any copy or interaction, ask:

> *"Would a great coach, standing next to me, say it this way?"*

If yes, it's in character. If it sounds like an app, a form, or a report — rewrite it.

## 8. The interface should feel like
- A warm cafe with sunlight in the morning
- A premium Japanese coffee bar
- An Apple Pro app
- A conversation with a World Brewers Cup champion

The interface should not feel like:
- Excel
- Power BI
- Bloomberg
- Hospital software
- Generic SaaS dashboards
